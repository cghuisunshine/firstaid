🔴 DEFINITELY ON EXAMBasicsWhat are the First Aid age categories?
Adult: over 8 years • Child: 1–8 years • Infant: under 1 year.
🔴 DEFINITELY ON EXAMLawWhat law protects a First Aid Attendant from liability at work?
The Workers’ Compensation Act.
🔴 DEFINITELY ON EXAMRolesWho is responsible for adequate First Aid supplies?
The employer — not the First Aid Attendant.
🔴 DEFINITELY ON EXAMRolesWhat work-duty recommendation can a First Aid Attendant make?
Recommend alternative duties and liaise with the employer/supervisor.
🔴 DEFINITELY ON EXAMMedicationWhat is an EpiPen used for?
A severe allergic reaction / anaphylaxis.
🔴 DEFINITELY ON EXAMMedicationIf the exam lists the rights of medication as choices, what answer did the instructor give?
ALL OF THE ABOVE.
🔴 DEFINITELY ON EXAMPPEWhich two PPE items did the instructor say to know for the exam?
Gloves and masks.
🔴 DEFINITELY ON EXAMInfectionWhat does “airborne” mean?
Bacteria/viruses that travel through the air.
🔴 DEFINITELY ON EXAMCommunicationWhat communication clue is definitely on the exam?
Body language — including gestures and facial expressions.
🔴 DEFINITELY ON EXAMConsentWhat is implied consent?
If the casualty becomes unresponsive/unalert/unconscious, you have implied consent to help.
🔴 DEFINITELY ON EXAMCPRWhat is the CPR compression-to-breath ratio?
30 compressions followed by 2 breaths — compressions first.
🔴 DEFINITELY ON EXAMSeizuresShould you restrain a casualty during an active seizure?
No. Do not restrict their movement; protect them and help after the convulsion.
🔴 DEFINITELY ON EXAMDressingsWhat are the characteristics of a good dressing?
Clean and long enough — per the instructor’s exam wording.
🔴 DEFINITELY ON EXAMBurnsWhat burn degree/term is indicated by blisters?
Partial-thickness / second-degree burn.
🔴 DEFINITELY ON EXAMBurnsHot coffee spilled on the leg causes what type of burn?
A thermal (heat) burn.
🔴 DEFINITELY ON EXAMBurnsMatch the burn terminology and degrees.
Superficial = 1st degree • Partial thickness = 2nd degree • Full thickness = 3rd degree.

Course study cards (151)

COURSE STUDYFirst AidWhat are the three objectives of First Aid, in order?
Preserve life → prevent further injury → promote recovery.
COURSE STUDYFirst AidWhat does “life over limb” mean?
Treat life-threatening problems before less urgent injuries.
COURSE STUDYCommunicationHow should you delegate a 911 call?
Identify a specific person, give clear instructions, and confirm they understand.
COURSE STUDYLawWhat protection was discussed for helping outside work?
Good Samaritan law/act. Instructor said this was not an exam item.
COURSE STUDYHandoverWhat does IMIST summarize?
Identify • Mechanism • Injuries/illnesses • Signs/symptoms • Treatment.
COURSE STUDYHandoverDo you need to memorize IMIST verbatim?
No. Know what information to hand over to EMS.
COURSE STUDYESMWhat are the 5 main Emergency Scene Management stages?
1 Scene Survey • 2 Primary Survey • 3 Transport Decision • 4 Secondary Survey • 5 Ongoing Care.
COURSE STUDYScene SurveyWhat four prompts are used in the Scene Survey?
What happened? • Is it safe? • How many? • Consent/AVPU?
COURSE STUDYSafetyWhose safety comes first at the scene?
The First Aider’s safety comes first.
COURSE STUDYConsentFor a responsive casualty, what does “no means no” mean?
Respect refusal of First Aid; call 911/stand by when emergency assistance is needed.
COURSE STUDYAVPUWhat does AVPU stand for in this course?
Alert • Voice • Pain • Unalert.
COURSE STUDYAVPUHow was pain response checked in this course?
A thumb pinch at the base of the thumb.
COURSE STUDYPrimary SurveyWhat does ABC stand for?
Airway • Breathing • Circulation.
COURSE STUDYAirwayHow is the airway opened?
Head-tilt/chin-lift.
COURSE STUDYBreathingHow long do you check breathing?
At least 5 seconds, but no more than 10 seconds.
COURSE STUDYBreathingWhat if there is no normal breathing or only irregular gasping?
Start CPR right away.
COURSE STUDYCirculationWhat circulation signs can you check?
Skin condition/temperature, fingertip refill, or colour inside the mouth.
COURSE STUDYCirculationWhat can blue/gray mouth tissue or fingertips suggest?
Poor circulation / poor oxygenation.
COURSE STUDYRBSWhat does RBS stand for?
Rapid Body Survey.
COURSE STUDYRBSWhy perform a Rapid Body Survey?
To check for hidden or less obvious injuries.
COURSE STUDYRBSWhat should you do while performing an RBS?
Communicate out loud and explain what you are doing, even if the casualty is unresponsive.
COURSE STUDYTransportWhat are the three Transport Decisions?
Rapid Transport • Medical Aid • Return to Work.
COURSE STUDYTransportWhat does Rapid Transport mean?
Call 911 / ambulance.
COURSE STUDYMedical AidWhat are the main Medical Aid actions?
Arrange transport • stabilize the injury • document.
COURSE STUDY911When should decreased level of consciousness trigger 911?
When the casualty is unconscious, unalert, or unresponsive.
COURSE STUDY911What ABC problems require 911?
Ineffective airway, breathing, or circulation.
COURSE STUDY911What RBS finding can require 911?
Multiple/significant injuries you cannot manage yourself.
COURSE STUDYERPWhat does ERP stand for?
Emergency Response Plan.
COURSE STUDYRapid TransportWhat equipment did the instructor ask a helper to bring?
First Aid kit and AED kit.
COURSE STUDYReturn to WorkWhat follows treatment when returning someone to work?
Advise on care, follow up, document, and recommend alternative duties if appropriate.
COURSE STUDYSecondary SurveyWhat three actions were listed for Secondary Survey?
Expose injury • examine/palpate • check circulation and nerve function.
COURSE STUDYSecondary SurveyWhy repeat a head-to-toe exam in Secondary Survey?
To make sure nothing was missed during the first body check.
COURSE STUDYOngoing CareHow often did the whiteboard say to reassess ABCs?
Every 5 minutes.
COURSE STUDYOngoing CareWhat if the casualty’s ABCs change?
Update 911 and reconsider/update the Transport Decision.
COURSE STUDYRecovery PositionWhy use the recovery position?
To help maintain an open airway in an unconscious breathing casualty.
COURSE STUDYReassuranceWhat is a good way to reassure a casualty?
“You’re not alone, and I’m here to help.” Do not promise everything will be okay.
COURSE STUDYMoving CasualtyIf a casualty is found on their stomach, what do you assess first?
Begin the survey in the position found; assess breathing for 5–10 seconds.
COURSE STUDYMoving CasualtyIf normal breathing is not detected while prone, what next?
Safely roll onto the back while protecting the head and neck, then continue Primary Survey.
COURSE STUDYMoving CasualtyWhich direction should you roll a casualty?
Toward you, while supporting/protecting the head and neck.
COURSE STUDYShockWhat is shock?
A condition in which the body does not get enough oxygenated blood.
COURSE STUDYShockWhy is shock dangerous?
It is progressive and can lead to unconsciousness and death if unmanaged.
COURSE STUDYShockWhat are common signs of shock?
Pale/gray or mottled skin, cool/clammy skin, fast shallow breathing, weakness, confusion.
COURSE STUDYShockWhat is the first principle of First Aid for shock?
Give First Aid for the underlying cause.
COURSE STUDYShockShould you give food or drink to a casualty in shock?
No. Moisten the lips if thirsty, but do not give food or drink.
COURSE STUDYFaintingWhat is fainting?
A brief loss of consciousness from a temporary shortage of oxygenated blood to the brain.
COURSE STUDYFaintingWhat are common warning signs of fainting?
Pale, sweating, nausea, dizziness, or feeling unsteady.
COURSE STUDYFaintingWhat should you do if someone feels faint?
Lie them flat if possible or sit them down; provide fresh air, loosen tight clothing, stay with them.
COURSE STUDYAnginaWhat is angina in the course explanation?
Temporary reduced blood flow to heart muscle causing discomfort, often relieved by rest/nitroglycerin.
COURSE STUDYHeart AttackWhat does heart-attack discomfort often feel like?
Broad discomfort or a crushing/squeezing sensation, not usually a sharp stabbing pain.
COURSE STUDYHeart AttackWhere can heart-attack pain/discomfort appear?
Upper body — from upper abdomen to lower jaw and arms.
COURSE STUDYHeart AttackWhat position is often comfortable for suspected heart attack/angina?
Semi-sitting, at rest.
COURSE STUDYNitroglycerinHow is nitroglycerin taken in this course?
Spray or tablet under the tongue; do not swallow the tablet.
COURSE STUDYASAWhat ASA dose did the video suggest when appropriate?
One regular ASA tablet or two low-dose ASA tablets, chewed.
COURSE STUDYASAWho should not receive ASA according to the instructor?
A casualty age 19 or younger, due to concern about Reye syndrome.
COURSE STUDYCardiac ArrestWhat is agonal breathing?
Gasping, irregular, ineffective breathing seen in cardiac arrest.
COURSE STUDYCardiac ArrestWhat should you do for cardiac arrest?
Begin CPR and use an AED/defibrillator as soon as possible.
COURSE STUDYCPRWhere do the hands go for adult CPR?
Midline on the breastbone, one hand on top of the other.
COURSE STUDYCPRWhat compression depth did the video teach?
At least 5 cm / 2 inches.
COURSE STUDYCPRWhat compression rate did the video teach?
100–120 compressions per minute.
COURSE STUDYCPRWhat should happen between compressions?
Allow the chest to fully recoil.
COURSE STUDYCPRHow much air should each ventilation deliver?
Just enough to make the chest rise; avoid over-ventilating.
COURSE STUDYCPRWhen can CPR stop?
AED applied, casualty breathes again, help takes over, or rescuer is too exhausted to continue effectively.
COURSE STUDYPanic AttackWhy are panic attacks discussed after heart attacks?
Their signs and symptoms can look similar to a heart attack.
COURSE STUDYPanic AttackWhat breathing method did the instructor recommend?
Box breathing: inhale 4 sec → hold 4 sec → exhale 4 sec.
COURSE STUDYPanic AttackShould you use a brown paper bag for panic breathing?
No. The instructor advised against it.
COURSE STUDYStrokeWhat does CVA stand for?
Cerebrovascular Accident — stroke.
COURSE STUDYTIAWhat does TIA stand for?
Transient Ischemic Attack.
COURSE STUDYStrokeWhat does FAST stand for?
Face droop • Arm drift • Speech problems • Time to get immediate medical help.
COURSE STUDYStrokeWhat is the biggest takeaway from FAST?
Time — call 911 immediately and note when symptoms started.
COURSE STUDYStrokeIf one side is paralyzed, how should recovery position be arranged?
Healthy/uninjured side down; damaged/paralyzed side up.
COURSE STUDYStrokeWhat should a stroke/TIA casualty receive by mouth?
Nothing by mouth; moisten lips if thirsty.
COURSE STUDYDiabetesWhat signs were linked to a high-blood-sugar emergency?
Flushed, dry, warm skin; groggy/drowsy; breath may smell like nail polish remover.
COURSE STUDYDiabetesIf you are unsure low vs high blood sugar and they can swallow safely, what did the video advise?
Give something sweet; sugar helps hypoglycemia and was said not to worsen hyperglycemia.
COURSE STUDYSeizuresWhat is an aura?
A sensation that may warn a casualty that a seizure is about to occur.
COURSE STUDYSeizuresWhat are the two phases of a generalized seizure?
Tonic phase (rigid/loss of consciousness) and clonic phase (convulsions).
COURSE STUDYSeizuresShould you put anything in a casualty’s mouth during a seizure?
No. Never place anything in the mouth.
COURSE STUDYSeizuresWhat is the postictal state?
The period after a seizure when the casualty may be confused and exhausted.
COURSE STUDYSeizuresWhen should medical aid be considered after a seizure?
Injury, unconscious >5 min, second seizure soon after, or first-ever seizure.
COURSE STUDYFever SeizureWhat fever thresholds did the video call emergencies?
Infant: 38°C / 100.5°F+ • Child: 40°C / 104°F+.
COURSE STUDYAsthmaWhat are common signs of a severe asthma attack?
Coughing, wheezing, respiratory distress, chest tightness, anxiety, bluish lips/extremities.
COURSE STUDYAsthmaWhat is the tripod position?
Sitting upright and leaning forward to make breathing easier.
COURSE STUDYAsthmaHow long should the casualty hold their breath after an inhaler dose?
About 10 seconds, if able.
COURSE STUDYAsthmaWhy use a spacer?
It holds aerosol medication so the casualty can inhale it over several breaths.
COURSE STUDYAnaphylaxisWhat are common signs of anaphylaxis?
Airway swelling, trouble breathing, hives, nausea/vomiting, lightheadedness, feeling of doom.
COURSE STUDYAnaphylaxisWhere is an epinephrine auto-injector placed?
Firmly into the fleshy outside of the mid-thigh.
COURSE STUDYOpioidsWhat are common signs of opioid poisoning?
Blue/gray/purple lips, pinpoint pupils, snoring/gurgling sounds, slow/no breathing, severe drowsiness/unconsciousness.
COURSE STUDYNaloxoneWhat does naloxone do?
Temporarily counteracts/reverses opioid poisoning.
COURSE STUDYNaloxoneWhy is hospital/medical follow-up still needed after naloxone?
The effect is temporary and can wear off.
COURSE STUDYNaloxoneWhat if the overdose scene feels unsafe?
Do not put yourself at risk; call 911 and document why you did not intervene.
COURSE STUDYHypothermiaWhat is normal core body temperature?
About 37°C.
COURSE STUDYHypothermiaHow is hypothermia rewarmed in the instructor’s comparison?
Gradually.
COURSE STUDYFrostbiteHow is frostbite rewarmed in the instructor’s comparison?
Rapidly, but only when you can keep the area warm afterward.
COURSE STUDYFrostbiteWhy should frostbitten tissue not be thawed if it may refreeze?
Refreezing after thawing can cause more damage.
COURSE STUDYHeat IllnessWhat are the three heat conditions discussed?
Heat cramps • heat exhaustion • heat stroke.
COURSE STUDYHeat StrokeWhich heat illness did the instructor call the most dangerous?
Heat stroke.
COURSE STUDYHeat IllnessWhat general care did the instructor describe for heat injuries?
Move out of heat, cool the casualty, and restore hydration/electrolytes when appropriate.
COURSE STUDYConcussionWhat visible clues can suggest concussion?
Motionless/slow to rise, unsteady, clutching head, dazed/vacant look, confusion, disorientation.
COURSE STUDYConcussionWhat symptoms can suggest concussion?
Headache, nausea/vomiting, dizziness, drowsiness, confusion, blurred vision, amnesia, light sensitivity.
COURSE STUDYConcussionWhat kind of memory/orientation questions should you ask?
Questions you already know the answer to: name, location, month, year, etc.
COURSE STUDYOSIWhat indicators of occupational stress injury were discussed?
Sleep disruption, fatigue, appetite loss, anxiety/panic, irritability, work disengagement, risk-taking, isolation.
COURSE STUDYOSIWhat four action ideas were emphasized for OSI?
Take action • engage in conversation • make meaningful connections • access help.
COURSE STUDYDressingsWhat goes directly on a wound?
A dressing.
COURSE STUDYBandagesWhat goes over the dressing?
A bandage, to hold the dressing in place and add pressure if needed.
COURSE STUDYBleedingWhat is pinpoint pressure?
Focused pressure with the fingertips to help control bleeding.
COURSE STUDYBandagesWhat can a triangular bandage be folded into?
A broad bandage, which can be used to make a loop tie.
COURSE STUDYBleedingWhich did the instructor say is more dangerous: arterial or venous bleeding?
Arterial bleeding.
COURSE STUDYAmputationHow did the instructor describe packaging an amputated part?
Cover with saline-moistened dressing, bag it, then place that bag with/in ice and label it.
COURSE STUDYInternal BleedingWhat signs can suggest internal bleeding?
Large bruising/discoloration, coughing/spitting/vomiting blood, blood in urine or stool.
COURSE STUDYInternal BleedingWhat is the First Aider’s main action for suspected internal bleeding?
Arrange medical transport/care; internal bleeding may need hospital/surgical treatment.
COURSE STUDYMinor WoundsWhat three functions were checked before treating the arm wound?
Circulation • sensory function • motor function.
COURSE STUDYMinor WoundsHow long did the video say wound closures should stay on?
7–10 days.
COURSE STUDYMinor WoundsWhat aftercare was given for a minor wound?
Keep dressing clean/dry, protect it in shower, and return for follow-up within 24–48 hours.
COURSE STUDYMinor WoundsWhat was the practice sequence for a small forearm cut?
Saline → clean gauze → discard dirty gauze → 2 butterfly closures → new gauze → roller bandage.
COURSE STUDYRecordsWhy complete a First Aid Record?
It documents what happened, assessment, treatment, and why decisions were made.
COURSE STUDYRecordsHow long must the First Aid Record be kept?
At least 3 years.
COURSE STUDYRecordsHow must First Aid Records be stored?
Confidentially at the employer’s workplace.
COURSE STUDYRecordsIs the First Aid Record automatically submitted to WorkSafeBC?
No. Keep it at the workplace; provide a copy if WorkSafeBC requests it.
COURSE STUDYRecordsWhat belongs in the injury/illness description?
What happened, signs/symptoms, what was seen/felt/heard, and all assessment findings.
COURSE STUDYRecordsWhat belongs in the treatment section?
Each assessment and treatment step, in order.
COURSE STUDYRecordsWhat if there are no witnesses?
Cross off/omit the witness section.
COURSE STUDYRecordsWhat should transport arrangements document?
Why they went, how, who drove, time, destination, and other relevant details.
COURSE STUDYRecordsWhat if the worker refuses or cannot sign?
Document the refusal or inability to sign.
COURSE STUDYBurnsWhat are the four main burn causes discussed?
Heat/thermal • chemical • electrical • radiation.
COURSE STUDYBurnsWhat makes burn severity important besides depth?
Size and location of the burn.
COURSE STUDYThermal BurnsWhat is a scald?
A heat/thermal burn caused by hot liquid or steam.
COURSE STUDYThermal BurnsWhat is the first action for a heat burn?
Cool it immediately with cool water or a clean wet cloth until pain lessens.
COURSE STUDYThermal BurnsWhat should be removed early before swelling?
Jewelry and tight clothing, unless stuck to the burn.
COURSE STUDYThermal BurnsShould you remove material stuck to a burn?
No.
COURSE STUDYChemical BurnsHow long should a chemical burn be flushed initially?
15–20 minutes with large amounts of cool water.
COURSE STUDYChemical BurnsWhat do you do first with a dry-powder chemical?
Brush off loose powder before flushing with water.
COURSE STUDYElectrical BurnsWhat is the first priority with an electrical burn?
Scene safety — make sure the electricity is off; never touch/move live wires.
COURSE STUDYElectrical BurnsWhat burns should you look for after current travels through the body?
Entry and exit burns.
COURSE STUDYElectrical BurnsWhat other injuries may occur after electric shock?
Cardiac arrest, head/spinal injury, fractures, or dislocations.
COURSE STUDYRadiation BurnsWhat is the most common radiation burn discussed?
Sunburn.
COURSE STUDYBurnsShould burn blisters be broken?
No. Breaking blisters may promote infection.
COURSE STUDYBurnsWhich burn types should be seen by medical aid?
Chemical and electrical burns; serious burns based on age, depth, size, location, or complications.
COURSE STUDYSprainsWhat is a sprain?
Damage to a ligament.
COURSE STUDYSprainsWhat does a ligament connect?
Bone to bone at a joint.
COURSE STUDYSprainsWhen did the instructor say medical referral may be needed?
No improvement after 3 days, or concerning symptoms such as numbness.
COURSE STUDYBack StrainWhat symptoms did the first aider ask about during the back assessment?
Pain location plus any numbness or tingling in the legs.
COURSE STUDYBack StrainWhat movements were used to check back range of motion?
Lean forward, backward, away from the first aider, and toward the first aider.
COURSE STUDYBack StrainWhat did limited mobility lead the first aider to recommend?
Medical referral — go and see a doctor.
COURSE STUDYBack StrainHow was ice used in the back-strain example?
Placed over the shirt for about 10 minutes.
COURSE STUDYBack StrainWhat happened after the back strain was judged to need medical aid?
Arrange transport and complete the First Aid Record while waiting.
COURSE STUDYAnkle ExamWhat assessment sequence did the instructor start with for an ankle sprain?
Scene Survey → ABCs → Rapid Body Survey.
COURSE STUDYAnkle ExamDuring the RBS after a fall, where should the first aider check?
From the head all the way down to the injured ankle, checking for other injuries.
COURSE STUDYAnkle ExamWhat should you feel for around the injured ankle during the RBS?
Pain and tenderness from the ankle area down toward the foot.
COURSE STUDYAnkle ExamHow was ankle motor/range-of-motion function checked?
Press down, up, side to side against resistance, then wiggle the toes.
COURSE STUDYAnkle ExamHow was sensation checked in the ankle examination?
Touch a toe and ask the casualty which toe is being touched.
COURSE STUDYAnkle ExamWhy compare the injured ankle with the uninjured ankle?
To compare with the casualty’s normal baseline for pain, sensitivity, strength, and movement.
COURSE STUDYAnkle ExamWhat final functional check did the instructor use?
Ask the casualty to stand and take four steps.
